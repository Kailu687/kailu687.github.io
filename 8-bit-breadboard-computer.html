8-bit Breadboard Computer — Building Computation from Logic Gates

Designed and built a portable, Ben Eater–inspired 8-bit computer using discrete TTL logic ICs across 14 breadboards and more than 330 jumper wires.

The system features 16 bytes of memory, hardware-based addition and subtraction of 8-bit operands, registers for data storage, and clock and control circuitry for coordinating operations.

My primary contributions were the power supply unit (PSU) and RAM subsystem, alongside collaborative hardware integration and debugging. The project offered hands-on insight into how computation, memory, and data movement can be implemented using individual digital logic components.

What I Worked On

  • Power supply: Designed and implemented the PSU and power distribution for the multi-board system.

  • RAM subsystem: Worked on the 74LS189-based memory design and its integration with the computer.

  • Hardware integration: Helped bring together the digital logic modules across the breadboards.

  • Debugging: Worked through wiring faults, short circuits, and unexpected logic states during testing.

ChallengesHow did we overcome it
Noise: Due to the proximity of any 2 given wires in the system, a value going through a wire caused interference in the neighbourhood of its path thus causing the circuit to go erratic for certain states. Switching between jumper wires and standard 20 AWG wires for better noise reliance and wire-clearance. 
Switching Voltage Drop: When switching from USB-C to LiPo , the voltage drops for a split second, which might cause the computer to lose its memory and halt operations. Added a capacitor between the computer's power inputs to handle the voltage drop. 
Complex wiring : Tracing individual connections and identifying wiring errors became difficult as the circuit grew.

Checked connections systematically, isolated suspect sections, and debugged the circuit incrementally.  

Short Circuits : Wiring mistakes and shorts made reliable operation and fault isolation more challenging.

Traced connections and checked the affected sections during integration and testing.  

Hardware Integration: Correct individual modules did not automatically guarantee correct behaviour across the complete system.Standardised inputs and outputs across different modules and made a common pathway (bus) that the data across the entire system could use.
